# R04-0001 primary-session separate adversarial review

- Reviewer: primary session in a separate adversarial pass under the
  product-owner instruction forbidding subagents and Fleet;
  actor_independence=false is recorded, not hidden.
- Identification is derived, never accepted. Confounding, collider
  conditioning, and temporal order are all assessed on every
  evaluation; an unadjusted or honestly unmeasured confounder, a
  conditioned collider, or a causal edge whose lag establishes no
  order each force NOT_IDENTIFIED, and a graph carrying assumptions
  can rise no higher than ASSUMPTION_DEPENDENT. A declared status
  above the derived one is refused with the failing assessments
  named, and a sealed gate cannot be upgraded afterwards even when
  its hash is recomputed.
- A purely correlational graph establishes no temporal order at all
  and therefore cannot be identified, which is the case an
  association-only pipeline is most likely to overclaim.
- The three inference modes stay separate. Each contributes exactly
  one verdict; a missing, duplicated, or unknown mode fails closed. An
  inductive synthesis carrying a causal verdict and an aporia record
  arriving with an explanation already selected are both refused as
  mode collapse. Modes can only lower the ceiling: a live competing
  explanation or a conditional deductive trace caps the result at
  ASSUMPTION_DEPENDENT and a broken trace caps it at NOT_IDENTIFIED,
  while no combination of favourable modes can lift a graph whose
  assessments failed.
- Both graphs are validated against their canonical schemas -
  mechanism-graph for the causal structure and argument-graph with its
  ScopeVector reference for the R02 trace - so the gate is bound to
  the shared contracts rather than to a local convention.
- Residual limitations: the engine assesses the graph it is given and
  does not discover structure, so a confounder nobody declared is
  invisible to it; backdoor reasoning is limited to declared confounder
  roles rather than full path enumeration; and promotion of an
  identified cause remains a governance decision. This review is not
  external actor-independent certification.
